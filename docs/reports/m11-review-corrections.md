# M11 Pull Request Review Corrections

## Purpose and Status

This report records the corrections made on October 20, 2026, under the maintainer's authorization, in response to the first CI run and the automated Codex review of pull request #15 at head `2aae483`. M11 remains accepted under Decision 45 within its recorded evidence limits; the corrections do not alter the qualification evidence, its source identifiers, or its recorded SHA-256 values. The implementation and focused tests were delegated to a subagent under a written design brief; the coordinating agent reviewed the diffs, strengthened one regression test, and verified the results independently.

## CI Failure of the Storage Device Test

CI run 39369860891 passed the release build and strict formatting on GitHub's macOS runner, and its Linux job passed, including the first Linux run of `RebuildCoreTests`, whose suites such as `FileInventoryTests`, `BackupIndependenceTests`, `TimestampTests`, and `RecoveryRecordTests` passed (measured). The macOS job failed one expectation of `StorageDeviceTests.systemVolumeDirectoryHasKnownPhysicalDisk()`: the runner reported no physical interconnect for its system volume (measured). `StorageDevice` reports every undeterminable fact as nil, and `BackupIndependence` treats the interconnect as informational, so the assumption was the test's. Commit `d5d21fb` removes that expectation only; the test still requires a known physical whole disk that differs from the volume's media, a local volume, and no disk image. A new test shows that the independence verdict is the same with and without an interconnect, and that an unknown physical disk is refused even when the interconnect is known. No production code changed for this correction.

## Catalog Albums Without Files

The review found that `CatalogMatcher.match` reports missing tracks only for albums with at least one matched file, a behavior that `apply` and `check` rely on. In `rebuild prepare`, a catalog album with no files under SOURCE therefore passed step 1. A regression test reproduces the complete case, in which the album is absent from the source, the Music library, and the backup: at the original head, preparation completed with status 0 and wrote a recovery record (measured in a temporary worktree). Commit `eb3208d` leaves the matcher unchanged and makes step 1 compare every track reference of the catalog with the matched references, reporting each absent track. The test requires refusal at step 1, with and without `--dry-run`, a reason for each of the four absent tracks, no restoration, no disconnection wait, and no recovery record.

## Unconfirmed Backup Disconnection

The review found that `MountState` treated a failure to create a DiskArbitration session, to read the mount table, or to read a mounted device's description or volume UUID as absence of the volume, so step 6 could pass and record a disconnection that was not observed (established by reading the original source). Commit `424409d` introduces a three-valued status: mounted when an entry has the requested UUID; unknown when the UUID is empty, when inspection fails, or when a mounted `/dev/` entry of type APFS or HFS+ has an unreadable identity, because the verified backup has one of those types; and not mounted otherwise. The bounded wait succeeds only after a confirmed absence and at its deadline reports a volume still mounted or the reason for an unknown result; `rebuild prepare` refuses step 6 in both cases without writing a recovery record. iLibrary still never unmounts the volume. Pure tests cover every status rule, a failing reader, and scripted wait sequences, including that an unknown result is never reported as an unmount; a command test covers the refusal. The disk-image integration test still observes mounting and disappearance. On the maintainer's Mac, all eight mounted `/dev/` APFS and HFS+ entries had readable volume UUIDs (measured), so the fail-closed rule does not refuse ordinary disconnections there; the session-creation and mount-table failure branches cannot be induced on a working system and are covered only through the injectable reader.
